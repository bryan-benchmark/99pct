# Mission-Aligned Pricing (Benchmark doctrine)

**Top sentence:**

> Mission determines what we subsidize. Value determines what we charge.

This is good capitalism when the flywheel works. The dangerous version is “mission means cheap.”

---

## Ideal vs trap

| Trap | Ideal |
|------|--------|
| Make everything as cheap as possible because healthcare training should be accessible | Make the **behavior you want abundant**; charge for the **scarce things that become more valuable because of that abundance** |
| Philanthropy wearing SaaS clothes | Platform economics: subsidize the side of the market you want to grow |
| Cost-plus (“voice costs $0.70 → charge $1”) | Transparent about **what customers pay for**, not your margin; capture a share of value created |
| Undercharge enterprise “for the mission” | Price discriminate by ability to pay and value received; enterprise surplus funds access for individuals |

---

## What the Mission wants abundant

Mission: **Increase the supply of excellent healthcare workers.**

Want enormous quantities of:

practice → competency evidence → improvement → validated capability

Charging per-practice seat fees (e.g. $40/learner per Challenge) puts friction **directly against** the Mission.

That does **not** mean Benchmark captures little value. **Move the toll booth.**

---

## Move the toll booth

The enterprise asset is not merely a simulation. It is **competency infrastructure**:

- standards encoded correctly  
- workforce measured against them  
- evidence retained  
- managers alerted  
- remediation orchestrated  
- scoring calibrated  
- content maintained  
- compliance handled  
- analytics produced  
- eventually performance/quality data connected back into training  

Organizations pay for organizing, validating, governing, and operationalizing abundance—not for locking the practice faucet.

### Classic platform analogies

| Platform | Abundant / subsidized | Paid scarce layer |
|----------|----------------------|-------------------|
| Google | Search | Ads / enterprise |
| LinkedIn | Professional identity | Talent / sales / premium |
| GitHub | Developer use | Org collaboration, security, governance |
| Canva | Individual creation | Teams / orgs |

Free/cheap isn’t generosity for its own sake. It **increases the value of the paid layer**.

### Benchmark version

```
Practice abundant
High-quality Challenge creation abundant
Competency evidence abundant
        ↓
Organizing / validating / governing / operationalizing → extraordinarily valuable
```

## Economic architecture

| Layer | Price posture |
|-------|----------------|
| Abundance (practice, contribute excellent Challenges) | Free / near-zero friction |
| Coordination | Paid |
| Customization | Paid |
| Trust / validation | Paid |
| Governance | Paid |
| Service (Benchmark humans) | Paid |
| Enterprise infrastructure | Paid |

Doctrine rule:

> Benchmark should never make the mission more expensive simply because the mission is succeeding.

- Another worker wants to practice → near-zero friction  
- Another expert wants to contribute excellence → near-zero friction  
- Org wants build / validate / customize / govern / measure / integrate / maintain / guarantee → charge for value  

---

## Transparent ≠ cost-plus

Costs don’t determine customer value.

If a $2 simulation prevents a trainer from spending 45 minutes on a mock call + documentation, immediate labor value may be $50–$150+ before better competency detection. Capture some of that—or leave money that should fund product, research, employees, distribution, and mission impact on the table.

**Transparent pricing** = clear about what is paid for.  
**Not** = reveal or price from your COGS.

## Stronger than normal seat SaaS

| Normal seat SaaS | Mission-aligned Benchmark |
|------------------|---------------------------|
| Customer uses more → vendor charges more | Customer gets more successful → unit economics can improve for both |
| License anxiety on employee #4,827 | Org pays $50K → $100K → $500K+ as infrastructure; trainers say “use the hell out of it” |
| Misaligned incentives | CFO approves contract; frontline maximizes practice |

---

## Free creates the moat (flywheel)

Public practice (patterns, not PHI/employer secrets) + creators + enterprise private standards/calibration → better universal scoring, easier Challenge creation, richer competency ontology → better enterprise product → enterprise revenue → better public Benchmark → more creators/learners.

```
Free practice → more attempts → better competency infrastructure
    → better enterprise product → enterprise revenue
    → better Benchmark → more free practice
```

**The mission isn’t sitting next to the business model. The mission is causing the business model to compound.**

---

## Price discrimination (excellent capitalism)

Do **not** undercharge enterprise because of the mission.

If Included Health receives ~$300K/year of economic value, charging ~$75K is mission-consistent—that surplus can fund rural nurse / student / hospice / independent educator / small clinic at $0–$20.

| Segment | Posture |
|---------|---------|
| Individual | Free |
| Independent educator | Very inexpensive |
| Small organization | Affordable self-service |
| Large organization | Substantial contract |
| Needs Benchmark humans | More |
| Needs governance / integration / validation | Considerably more |

Extract more surplus from high willingness-to-pay / high value-received; preserve access for low WTP/ATP.

---

## Anti-patterns (bad capitalism in mission clothing)

1. “Mission means everything is cheap”  
2. Cost-plus as the pricing story  
3. Per-practice tolls that throttle the Mission numerator  
4. Underpricing enterprise and starving the flywheel  
5. Confusing accessibility with refusing to capture value where infrastructure is scarce
