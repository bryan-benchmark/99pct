## Core idea

Missionism is **not** primarily “employee ownership,” “Slicing Pie forever,” or “a democratic company.”

> **Missionism is a system for designing companies so that the rational self-interest of the contributor, the company, and the customer points in the same direction.**

Ownership is one main tool.  
Mission accounting is another.  
Human-burden accounting is another.  
Transparent prospective rules are another.  

Everything else is implementation.

---

## Preamble

Humans usually want better lives for themselves and for the people they care about.

Markets are powerful because they allow people to improve their own lives by creating things other people voluntarily value.

But markets often fail when important costs are hidden, power becomes concentrated, ownership becomes disconnected from contribution, or it becomes more profitable to extract value than to create it.

Missionism is an attempt to improve that system.

A Missionism company is still a company.

It may make enormous profits.  
It may compete aggressively.  
It may hire and fire.  
It may fail.  
It may raise capital.  
Its founders, employees, and investors may become extremely wealthy.

Missionism does not require people to become less ambitious.  
It tries to make ambition useful.

The central design question is:

> **If every person behaves rationally in their own interest, does the system cause the mission, the company, and the people it serves to become better?**

Missionism seeks a **positive-sum equilibrium** in which:

- **The contributor wins** by creating useful value.  
- **The company wins** because useful value makes the institution stronger.  
- **The customer or beneficiary wins** because the value was actually useful to them.

When those three incentives diverge, the system is unfinished.

# Part I — The Immutable Laws

These define Missionism itself.

Specific formulas may evolve. **These laws should not.**

## Law 1 — Every Missionism company exists for a measurable mission

Every company must publicly state the change in the world it exists to create.

Normally expressible as:

> **Increase ______.**  
> or  
> **Decrease ______.**

Examples: Increase clinician autonomy. Increase the global supply of excellent healthcare workers. Decrease healthcare logistics friction.

Revenue, profit, market capitalization, employee count, or defeating competitors are **not** sufficient missions. They may be useful consequences of advancing one.

---

## Law 2 — The mission must define what “better” means

Every Missionism company maintains durable directional **Arrows**.

Arrows answer: if we are actually succeeding, which things should generally move up and which should move down?

Examples: Quality ↑ · Access ↑ · Autonomy ↑ · Safety ↑ · Time required ↓ · Cost ↓ · Human burden ↓ · Unwanted switching friction ↓ · Evidence confidence ↑

The mission is very durable. The arrows are also durable. The exact equation representing those arrows may evolve more frequently.

---

## Law 3 — Mission progress must be measured honestly

Every Missionism company maintains a public or appropriately transparent **Mission Equation** — its current best attempt to measure progress.

At minimum it must consider: positive outcomes, reach, duration, evidence, causal attribution, material externalities, and hard boundaries.

Internal activity may not be called “mission progress” merely because it is profitable.

---

## Law 4 — Material human costs may not be treated as free

If the company requires humans to absorb meaningful physical danger, sleep disruption, schedule unpredictability, emotional trauma, cognitive burden, professional liability, loss of autonomy, or comparable personal exposure, that burden must be acknowledged **before** harm occurs.

Missionism rejects: “Nothing bad happened, therefore the risk cost nothing.”

Human exposure must influence the contributor’s economics or rights and must be visible as a cost to the organization.

---

## Law 5 — Contribution must retain a path to real ownership

Qualifying contributors must have a credible path to economic ownership in the institution they help build.

Ownership may be unequal. Roles and performance may differ enormously.  
Ownership may **not** permanently become a privilege available only to founders, investors, or people who arrived generations earlier.

---

## Law 6 — The rules must be known before the work is judged

Material compensation, contribution, mission-scoring, and ownership rules must be defined prospectively.

Future rules may improve. Past work may not conveniently be rescored because leadership dislikes the outcome.

> **Improve tomorrow’s ruler. Do not rewrite yesterday’s ruler.**

Fraud and factual errors may be corrected through a published process.

---

## Law 7 — Validly earned ownership is real

Once a contributor has validly earned ownership, it is not merely a discretionary bonus.

Subject to the legal security and its stated terms, it carries the rights attached to that ownership (distributions, sale proceeds, voting, appreciation, and the possibility of loss).

A contributor may leave or be fired. Future earning may stop.  
Previously earned ownership does not disappear merely because the relationship ended.

---

## Law 8 — Individual excellence and collective excellence must both matter

Missionism rejects two extremes:

- Purely collective reward → free riding  
- Purely individual reward → hoarding, internal competition, local optimization  

Must preserve both:

> **How much did I contribute?**  
> **How well did we advance the mission together?**

The exact formula may evolve. The dual incentive should remain.

---

## Law 9 — Hidden externalities belong inside the decision

A company may not intentionally improve its Mission Score by pushing material costs onto employees, customers, families, communities, future contributors, the environment, public systems, or other third parties and simply excluding those costs from consideration.

Not every externality can be perfectly quantified. It must at least be made visible.

---

## Law 10 — Missionism must remain corrigible

Missionism does not claim today’s formulas are perfect.

Every company must maintain a process for improving its Mission Equation, exposure model, contribution model, evidence standards, and era targets.

Changes become harder as they move closer to purpose:

| Class | Burden |
|-------|--------|
| Experiment | Easy |
| Era target | Moderately easy |
| Equation | Requires evidence |
| Arrow | Requires strong evidence |
| Mission | Extraordinary change |

Evolutionary speed limit.

---

## Law 11 — No person is the mission

Founders, Mission Leaders, and CEOs matter. None is the permanent source of legitimacy.

The company must have durable mission stewardship and succession beyond one person.

---

## Law 12 — Capital may finance the mission but may not secretly redefine it

Capital is valuable. Capital may earn returns. Investors may receive legitimate protections. Missionism does not require capital to behave altruistically.

Financing may not quietly eliminate mission obligations, contribution-to-ownership pathways, or other constitutional protections while still calling the company Missionism.

---

## Law 13 — A Missionism company may die

Missionism does not preserve bad companies.

Companies may fail, run out of money, go bankrupt, merge, be acquired, wind down, or complete their missions. Equity may become worthless.

Missionism protects the integrity of the game. It does not guarantee the game succeeds.

# Part II — The Triple-Win Test

Every meaningful policy, experiment, compensation system, product decision, or constitutional change should pass:

### Human

> Does the contributor become personally better off for making the right thing happen?

(cash, ownership, safety, autonomy, time, future opportunity, skills, burden, downside)

### Company

> Does the company become stronger when the right thing happens?

(profitability, resilience, capital efficiency, talent, productivity, Mission Velocity, innovation, EV)

### Customer / Beneficiary

> Does the person the mission exists for receive more value?

(quality, price, access, autonomy, safety, time, choice, friction, outcomes)

Ideal: **Human ↑ · Company ↑ · Customer ↑**

If one goes down, first ask:

> **Can we redesign the mechanism so the loser stops losing?**

That is Missionism’s default form of problem solving.

Full compiler: [DECISION_COMPILER.md](./DECISION_COMPILER.md).

# Part III — The Full Decision Compiler

Material decisions receive a standard **Mission Decision Record**.

Lenses include: Human · Company · Customer · Mission arrows · Human exposure · Externalities · Capital · Freedom/exit · Future generations · Resilience · Reversibility · Evidence · Gaming · Scale (10,000×) · Time (10 years) · Opportunity cost · Mission drift.

Purpose is **not** a magic score. It is to expose tradeoffs humans tend to ignore.

AI is a **mechanism-design copilot**, not a sovereign judge.

# Part IV — Human Contribution Accounting (recommended v1)

Keep contribution understandable. Exact mathematics are **to be tested**, not revealed truth.

```text
Contribution ≈ Base × HumanExposure + Impact
```

*(Form is illustrative; companies may test alternatives prospectively.)*

### Base

What does competent performance of this role contribute?  
Skill, scope, autonomy, responsibility, scarcity where relevant, consequence, leverage, time.  
**Boring and explainable.**

### Human Exposure

What are we asking this person to personally absorb merely by doing the job correctly?  
Physical hazard, time-control loss, on-call, night work, schedule unpredictability, emotional intensity, cognitive burden, professional/legal consequence, surveillance, meaningful loss of autonomy.  
Measured **before** harm occurs.

### Impact

What did this person make materially better beyond expected role contribution?

| Level | Meaning |
|-------|---------|
| **I0** | Expected contribution — Base covers this |
| **I1** | Local improvement |
| **I2** | Constraint removal for a larger system |
| **I3** | Era-variable impact |
| **I4** | Mission breakthrough (company-level Mission Velocity) |
| **I5** | Era-defining breakthrough |

Impact should be nonlinear. Exact multipliers remain empirical.

# Part V — The Human Exposure Principle

> **No human exposure is free.**

| Side | Effect |
|------|--------|
| **Worker** | Unavoidable exposure ↑ → contribution economics ↑ |
| **Company / mission** | Same exposure ↑ → efficiency / mission-positivity ↓ |

Therefore: workers are rewarded for carrying unavoidable burden; owners are rewarded for eliminating avoidable burden.

Compatible selfish incentives: accurate reporting · elimination Impact · safer mission.

Realized harm: ordinary wages, insurance, workers’ compensation, legal remedies. Also informs future exposure scoring.  
**Risk priced before harm. Consequences paid after harm.**

---

# Part VI — Eras

Hierarchy:

> **Mission → Arrows → Value Equation → Era → Experiments**

| Layer | Speed |
|-------|--------|
| Mission | Decades |
| Arrows | Very durable |
| Value Equation | Current best math of the arrows |
| Era | Bounded period with known targets and economics |
| Experiments | Constant search |

> Arrows change slower than equations.  
> Equations change slower than Era targets.  
> Experiments change constantly.

# Part VII — Ownership (public explanation)

> You get paid normally for working here.  
> You can also earn real ownership.  
> Doing harder or more valuable work can earn more.  
> Creating exceptional improvement can earn much more.  
> When the company advances its mission unusually well, contributor ownership can become available faster.  
> What you validly earn remains yours.

Cash pays for today’s life. Ownership is a stake in the future they help create.  
Missionism does not require people to work for free.

---

# Part VIII — Founder Ownership

Recommend founder-led beginnings — not democracy on Day 1.

Founders may begin with essentially all ownership: they created the institution, took initial risk, and need authority to hire, fire, pivot, and execute.

Recommended additional commitment:

> **A meaningful portion of founding ownership becomes earnable by contributors over time.**

A slow time path guarantees movement. Mission performance may accelerate it.

Exact schedule is **not yet universal law**. Possible lab model: slow ~30 years; exceptional ~10–15 years.

---

# Part IX — Mining (recommended framing)

Explain ownership as **mining**, not dilution theater.

| Concept | Meaning |
|---------|---------|
| **Mined** | What I already earned |
| **Mineable** | What contributors can earn this Era |
| **Reserve** | What remains for future contributors |

Illustrative:

```text
Your Era Ownership = Era Ownership Release × Your Contribution Share
Era Ownership Release = Time Release + Mission Acceleration
```

**Individual performance** → your share.  
**Collective performance** → how much can be earned.

That is the core flywheel.

---

# Part X — Profit

Missionism is not anti-profit.

| Question | Lens |
|----------|------|
| Are we improving what we promised? | **Mission Value** |
| Can we sustain and compound? | **Financial Value** |
| Who participates in consequences? | **Ownership** |

Seek strategies where Mission Value ↑ · Customer Value ↑ · Financial Value ↑.

Profit ≠ proof of mission success. Mission success without sustainable economics may also fail. Goal: alignment.

---

# Part XI — Capital

Capital ≠ human contribution. Do not mix the ledgers.

Prefer where practical: retained earnings, customer financing, debt, revenue/profit participation, redeemable or finite-return capital, and structures that let capital leave after a strong return.

Permanent equity is allowed when genuinely required.

Key question:

> Does the financing advance the mission without giving capital unilateral power to nullify the Missionism system?

---

# Part XII — Governance

More founder- and management-led than some earlier designs implied.

CEO should hire, fire, allocate, build/close products, price, sell, and run the company. **Not operational democracy.**

Mission Board (or equivalent) is **narrow**: protect mission, arrows, Value Equation integrity, Era boundaries, major expansions, succession.

Voting power should increasingly reflect real earned ownership rather than an artificial parliament when practical.

Layered law and three-key patterns for constitutional change: [CONSTITUTIONAL_LAYERS.md](./CONSTITUTIONAL_LAYERS.md). Company tests (e.g. GoPrivate) may add Houses; they are not required of every Missionism company by Part I.

# Part XIV — Ten Stress Tests

Same principles → different mechanisms. Train judgment; do not copy blindly.

1. **Capital-intensive frontier** — Capital gets strong financial rights; contributors earn for collapsing uncertainty / capital efficiency. Don’t pretend labor ownership replaces capital.  
2. **Breakthrough biotech** — Impact follows dependency graph; don’t crown one name for hundreds of contributors.  
3. **Highly automated software** — Reward automating one’s own work; Exposure for remaining real burden. Don’t reward preserving manual work.  
4. **High-turnover seasonal** — Pooled vehicles OK; track contribution individually.  
5. **Professional partnership** — Impact for teaching, systems, automation, making others more productive — not bureaucracy for its own sake.  
6. **Dangerous industry** — Price Exposure before injury; Impact for eliminating hazard.  
7. **Profit vs mission** — Keep Financial and Mission Value separate; don’t assume ownership alone prevents exploitation of captivity.  
8. **Irreplaceable superstar** — Preserve direct economics for unique personal output; reward amplifiers/scalers.  
9. **Incomparable roles** — Base vs role · Exposure vs burden · Impact vs documented mission change — no universal “nurse = 1.7 engineers.”  
10. **Illiquid private company** — Credible liquidity over time; don’t pretend early borrowing against private stock solves life.

# Part XV — Three examples (sketch)

### GoPrivate — Increase clinician autonomy

Arrows: clinician control ↑ · earnings ↑ · patient access ↑ · practice survival ↑ · unwanted admin ↓ · switching friction ↓ · human burden ↓ · cost of independence ↓  

Triple-Win: automate payer credentialing → clinician hours ↓ · GoPrivate cost ↓ · patients get practices faster.  

Anti-Missionism: punitive lock-in (profit ↑, autonomy ↓).

### Benchmark — Increase excellent healthcare workers

Arrows: excellent worker-years ↑ · readiness ↑ · competence ↑ · evidence ↑ · access ↑ · durability ↑ · creation cost ↓ · trainer/learner burden ↓ · safety risk ↓  

Triple-Win: SME + engineer cut validated sim creation 8h → 30m.  

Anti-Missionism: redefine “excellent” without independent evidence.

### NextBed — Decrease healthcare logistics friction

Arrows: time-to-placement ↓ · unused capacity ↓ · patient delay ↓ · travel/admin burden ↓ · safety/reliability ↑ · cost per transfer ↓  

Triple-Win: automate unsafe overnight coordination.  

Anti-Missionism: data captivity as retention.

# Part XVI — The Missionism Meta-Test

Every mechanism must answer:

> **If everybody understands this rule and selfishly optimizes it, what happens?**

Keep if: better reporting, safer work, knowledge sharing, automation, customer value, Mission Velocity, EV, legitimate ownership.

Fix before scaling if: hazard farming, Impact cartels, fake metrics, job preservation for its own sake, empire-building, captivity, short-term extraction, manipulation of future rules.

---

# Part XVII — What Missionism Is Not Yet Sure About

**Missionism Lab questions** — not immutable truth:

Base/Impact mix · Exposure multiplier structure · founder-transition speed · fast/slow clocks · Era length · Mission Board structure · capital instruments · mature-stage ownership renewal · liquidity mechanisms · company-wide voting architecture · Impact attribution algorithms

Companies test. Missionism collects evidence. Better mechanisms replace weaker ones **prospectively**.

---

# Part XVIII — The Short Constitution

> Pick a measurable mission.  
> Define what better means.  
> Pay people normally for their work.  
> Give contributors a real path to ownership.  
> Recognize human burden before people get hurt.  
> Reward exceptional improvements disproportionately.  
> Make collective mission success increase collective opportunity.  
> Keep customer, contributor, and company incentives pointed the same way.  
> Expose externalities instead of hiding them.  
> Set the rules before the work.  
> Improve future rules instead of rewriting past ones.  
> Let companies fail when they deserve to fail.  
> Never assume one leader, one generation, one investor, or one formula knows everything forever.

The central equation is not really an MCU formula:

```text
Good Mechanism  ⇒  Human ↑ · Company ↑ · Customer ↑ · Mission ↑
```

Keep redesigning until those arrows align as closely as reality permits.

That is the constitution Missionism builds around now.
