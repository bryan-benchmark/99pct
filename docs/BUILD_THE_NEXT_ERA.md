# Missionism

## Build the next era. Own part of what you create.

**Public explainer.** Doctrine: [`../spec/CONSTITUTION.md`](../spec/CONSTITUTION.md) · Certification: [`../spec/CERTIFICATION.md`](../spec/CERTIFICATION.md) · Market: [`../spec/MISHYS.md`](../spec/MISHYS.md) · Site: `/system`

---

Most startups grow by selling pieces of the company to investors.

Missionism offers another path:

> **Use ownership to reward the humans who create the next round of value.**

We still use normal companies, normal shares, normal salaries, independent private-company valuations, profits, boards, debt, and contracts.

The difference is what unlocks new ownership.

---

## How it works

Every Missionism company publishes its next **Era** before the work begins. An Era is expected to represent roughly five years of progress, but it ends when the gates are actually achieved.

An Era has:

| Element | Question |
|---------|----------|
| **Mission Gate** | Did we measurably make the thing we exist to improve better? |
| **Financial Gate** | Did an independent valuation show substantial new durable company value? |
| **Integrity Gates** | Without unacceptable human harm, customer captivity, fraud, safety failures, or hidden externalities? |
| **Contributor Mine** | What share \(s\) of *new verified aligned value* can contributors earn? |

Mine progress tracks the **weaker** gate: \(\mathrm{Progress}=\min(\mathrm{Mission\%},\mathrm{Financial\%})\). Full win requires both at 100% + Integrity PASS.

Precise economics: [`../spec/ERA_ECONOMICS.md`](../spec/ERA_ECONOMICS.md).

### Example

> Company starts Era at **$1 billion**.  
> Next financial target is **$5 billion**.  
> $4 billion of new value is available to create.  
> Company precommits **20% of that new aligned value** (\(s=20\%\)) to contributors.  
> Maximum Era Mine = **$800M**.

If the Era succeeds (both gates + integrity):

| Slice | Amount |
|-------|--------|
| **Contributors** | $800M |
| **Existing owners** | Keep ~$4.2B claim on a $5B company (started at $1B) |

Diluted percentages; richer owners. Customers had to become better off for the Era to count. Then the next Era begins.

## The offer to a worker

A normal company says:

> **We'll pay you $200,000 to do this job.**

A Missionism company says:

> **We'll pay you competitively.**  
> **And here is the next problem we're trying to solve.**  
> **Here is the independently measured financial upside.**  
> **Here is the mission outcome required.**  
> **Here is the ownership pool available if we win.**  
> **Here is exactly how your contribution earns a share of it.**

You don't receive equity because management likes you.  
You earn it through a published system.

Someone joining 30 years later gets the same basic offer:

> **You can't mine yesterday's value. You can own part of creating tomorrow's.**

---

## Why might this beat venture capital?

Traditional venture capital is extremely useful when a company needs money before it can create value.

The bargain is roughly:

> **Investor risks capital → investor receives ownership.**

Missionism introduces a second bargain:

> **Humans create new value → humans receive ownership.**

That can produce several advantages:

| Advantage | Why |
|-----------|-----|
| **Better talent** | Great people can get more upside than a normal employee without becoming founders |
| **Better cooperation** | Your ownership rises when other teams succeed |
| **Better hiring discipline** | Every hire shares the mine — is this person Era-accretive? |
| **Less dependence on VC** | Growth via customers, debt, retained earnings, ingenuity — not only selling ownership to capital |
| **Customers in the incentive system** | Financial growth alone does not unlock the mine; the mission must improve too |
| **No final generation** | New value creates new ownership opportunity forever |

## What stays exactly like capitalism?

A lot.

Companies still compete. People still negotiate salaries. Owners still want shares to appreciate. Companies still need profit. Equity still dilutes. Shares can still become worthless. Bad companies still fail. Private companies still need independent valuations. Investors can still invest. Employees can still quit or be fired.

Missionism isn't trying to remove self-interest.

> **It is trying to point self-interest in a more useful direction.**

---

## What is genuinely different?

In a conventional company, an employee can create $1 billion of new value and still receive roughly the same salary and option grant they negotiated when they joined.

Missionism explicitly asks:

> **Who created the new pie?**

and reserves some of that incremental pie for those contributors.

The old owners still win because they keep most of what was created.

### The central bargain

> # Create $5. Keep $1. Make everyone richer.

The exact ratio might be 10%, 20%, 25%, or vary by Era. Missionism should learn what produces the strongest long-term behavior.

But the rules are declared **before** the work.

---

## What could be worse?

Real disadvantages:

| Risk | Why it matters |
|------|----------------|
| **Dilution is real** | Existing owners own a smaller % after successful Eras |
| **Measurement is difficult** | Company value is easier than clinician autonomy or excellent-worker supply |
| **People will game the gates** | Large rewards pressure metrics, valuations, timing, attribution |
| **Equity is risky** | Private ownership is not cash; it can be worth zero |
| **Long-term work can be underrewarded** | Poor Eras can chase unlocks over fifteen-year investments |
| **Complexity** | Salaries are simple; attributing billions of new value is not |
| **Sometimes capital is scarce** | Fabs and biotech may still need billions of outside equity |

That’s why the system needs independent valuation, transparent mission equations, long-horizon measurements, auditability, and prospective rules.

## The public promise

Every certified Missionism company should be able to make this offer publicly:

> ## Here is our mission.  
> **Here is what better means.**  
> **Here is what our company is independently worth today.**  
> **Here is the next Era we are trying to win.**  
> **Here are the financial and mission gates required to win it.**  
> **Here are the human and customer protections we cannot violate to get there.**  
> **Here is the ownership available to the people who create that value.**  
> **Here is exactly how contribution is measured.**  
> **Here is our historical record of what previous generations actually earned.**

And then the invitation:

> # Pick a mission. Find the problem you can solve. Help grow the pie. Own part of what you create.

That is why Missionism is both **very different from venture capitalism** and **built from many of the same economic tools that already work.**
