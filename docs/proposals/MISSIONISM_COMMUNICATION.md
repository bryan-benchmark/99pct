# Missionism — Communication & Landing Principles

**Status:** Proposed  
**Maturity:** Experimental  
**Not Canonical.**

**Related:** locked surface copy in [`src/content/voice.ts`](../../src/content/voice.ts)

---

## Goal

A normal person should understand what Missionism is in **under 10 seconds**, without needing economics, political theory, organizational design jargon, or the history of capitalism.

Missionism should feel: practical, optimistic, human, useful, ambitious but not utopian — compatible with companies, markets, leaders, founders, investors, and employees.

Do **not** frame Missionism primarily as a movement, ideology, political philosophy, anti-capitalist project, cooperative movement, or alternative economic system.

Primary mental model:

> **Missionism is a better way to organize work.**

---

## Definition discipline

Any surface that answers “What is Missionism?” must use the locked public definition from `src/content/voice.ts` **verbatim** or link to a page that does.

Do not paraphrase the core definition differently page by page.

Supporting copy may explain Missionism, but it must not introduce a competing category such as:

- movement
- philosophy
- ideology
- economic model / economic system
- governance model (as the top-level category)
- employee-ownership model (as the only category)

Category hierarchy when needed:

1. A better way to organize work
2. An operating system for organizations
3. An organizational system
4. An open standard for mission-driven organizations (certification contexts)

---

## Ruthless simplicity rule

This is not an additive rewrite.

Prefer deleting, merging, and shortening existing content over adding explanatory copy.

Each homepage section should communicate **one idea**.

A reader should be able to skim only the headings and bold lines and still understand Missionism.

Avoid answering objections before the reader plausibly has them.

Do not introduce vocabulary merely because it exists elsewhere in the system.

**The homepage earns curiosity. The deeper pages satisfy it.**

Success test:

> After reading only the hero + section headings, could someone explain Missionism to their spouse at dinner without using any Missionism-specific vocabulary?

---

## Identity rule

People should not have to become “Missionists.”

Prefer: *Run your organization on Missionism* / *Build with Missionism* / *This company operates using Missionism.*

Comparable mental models: open source, Lean, Agile, Linux, a corporate structure, an operating system — not a religion, political party, or ideological identity.

Keep the “-ism”; neutralize it immediately by attaching a practical category.

---

## Three concepts

| Public | Technical triad |
|--------|-----------------|
| Own it | OWNERSHIP |
| Steer it | AGENCY |
| Serve the mission | ALIGNMENT |

Generational ownership (“built for generations”) is an extension of **Own it**, not a separate philosophy.

---

## Tone rules

- Start from someone’s working life, not theory.
- Prefer concrete before abstract.
- Explain mechanisms, not moral superiority.
- Do not attack capitalism, founders, investors, or executives as villains.
- Describe structural incentives: **Don’t blame the person. Design the system better.**
- Do not require heroism: **Good systems shouldn’t require heroes.**
- Keep **We come in peace.**
- Short sentences. Ordinary words: work, own, build, steer, mission, responsibility, evidence, contribution.
- Progressive disclosure: 10s → 1m → 5m → deep pages. Do not force the 30-minute explanation into the first 30 seconds.

---

## Homepage sequence (target)

1. Hero  
2. Why this matters  
3. Own it / Steer it / Serve the mission  
4. What changes  
5. What doesn’t change  
6. Built for generations  
7. We come in peace / go deeper  

Certification is **downstream infrastructure**. Do not use “Missionism Certified” to define the category on the homepage.

---

## Avoid list (primary marketing)

movement · ideology · economic system · philosophy · revolution · democratization of capitalism · socialism / capitalism comparisons · stakeholder capitalism · labor/proletariat · socioeconomic · post-capitalism · left/right/centrist labels

Those may appear in deeper educational material with concrete mechanic distinctions — never as the opening category.

---

## Desired visitor reaction

> “Wait. Why don’t companies already work this way?”

Not:

> “I need to read a manifesto to figure out whether I agree with these people.”
