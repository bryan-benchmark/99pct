# Reduce the Pathways to War — candidate Mission

**Status:** Proposed Mission idea, 2026-09-27. This is an exploration under [Mission Idea Method v0](./MISSION_IDEA_METHOD_V0.md), not an approved Mission, operating mandate, or change to `spec/canonical.json`.

## Human outcome and Mission kernel

People in and between countries can resolve escalating disputes without organized violence. The durable objective is to reduce the incidence and severity of war and the conditions that make escalation more likely, while protecting the agency and safety of affected communities.

This objective is larger than any one concert, campaign, or policy. The user's proposed mechanism combines **cross-border concerts and cultural gatherings** with **coordinated, locally accountable advocacy in many countries at once**. Cultural events could create relationships, attention, and resources; advocates could turn specific de-escalation proposals into public decisions. Whether either mechanism changes conflict risk is a hypothesis to test, not an assumed outcome.

## System map

Inputs include local artists and organizers, affected communities, researchers, funders, venues, advocates, decision makers, and public attention. Activities include listening and convening, public performances, evidence gathering, policy design, lawful advocacy, and follow-up. Outputs include participation, documented proposals, meetings, commitments, and public reports. Feedback must include affected people's assessments, independent evidence, unwanted effects, and a route to stop or revise a campaign.

The strongest inherited assumption to question is that peace efforts must stay confined to one country's audience or policy process. Cross-border coordination might make shared interests visible and reduce duplication. It could also create foreign-influence concerns, distort local priorities, or put participants at risk. Local partners must retain authority over their participation and messages.

## Three possible architectures

| Architecture | Mechanism | Main risk to test |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Concert network | Locally produced events connected by a shared peace objective, with proceeds and attention directed to locally selected work | Large audiences without any durable change, plus participant safety and funding concerns |
| Policy coalition | Independent advocates in multiple jurisdictions coordinate around a small, public set of de-escalation proposals, with local approval and disclosures | A common message that does not fit local needs or decision processes |
| Combined network | Concerts recruit and fund a transparent coalition; advocates report what was requested, spent, decided, and learned | Entertainment and advocacy become confused, or funders gain undue control |

The combined network is the concept to investigate first because it contains the user's full idea. A reversible pilot should test one link in its causal chain before scaling simultaneous campaigns globally.

## First reversible test

Recruit independent partners in two or three willing locations around **one specific, locally selected de-escalation issue**. Before any public event or advocacy, each partner records the intended audience, proposal, local decision authority, budget, participant safeguards, disclosure approach, and stop rule. Run one small cultural convening and a time-limited, partner-led advocacy effort. Publish a plain-language ledger of funding, activities, and decisions, subject to participant privacy and safety.

Measure what the team can directly observe: participation and repeat engagement across groups, funds raised and spent, locally endorsed proposals, decision-maker meetings, public commitments, and follow-through. Track possible harm, including intimidation, exclusion, misrepresentation, or backlash. Conflict incidence is the long-term outcome, but a short pilot cannot claim it reduced war. Compare results with a partner-led policy coalition that does not use a concert, or another credible local alternative. Stop if a local partner withdraws, safety incidents cannot be managed, disclosures or required approvals are unresolved, or the work becomes a vehicle for a party, state, or funder to override affected people.

## Governance and evidence questions

- Who from conflict-affected communities has actual authority over goals, framing, spending, and stopping work? How are dissenting voices represented?
- Which proposals have a plausible causal path to lower escalation, and what evidence would make the team change course?
- Who funds the events and advocates, and how are interests, payments, meetings, and outcomes disclosed?
- What independent local review is needed before advocacy, fundraising, cross-border transfers, public events, or work in sensitive jurisdictions? Obtain jurisdiction-specific professional advice before operations.
- How will the team protect participants, performers, and local partners from unwanted exposure or retaliation?
- What evidence distinguishes cultural reach from durable cooperation or policy change? Record claims as observed, supported, hypothesis, or unknown, with source and date.

**Current evidence status:** The outcome and combined mechanism are a proposal from the founder conversation. No demand, effectiveness, safety, feasibility, partner commitment, or legal clearance is established by this document.

## Founder decision before an operating pilot

**Recommended:** Treat this as a separately governed Mission with a public funding and advocacy ledger. Each participating local partner approves its own goals and messages and can stop its participation. Begin with one de-escalation issue in a few willing locations before considering simultaneous global advocacy. This makes the concerts and advocacy accountable to affected people, but it slows coordination and may prevent one uniform worldwide campaign. The founder should decide whether that local authority is a condition of the Mission; product software cannot make that governance choice.

## Product boundary

This could later become a Mission using Sparks, pilots, Team-Ups, and auditable events. A Spark could state the first test; a pilot could record its steward, resources, consent, measures, and stop rule; Team-Ups could document partner authority and commitments. The current B2B Mission Workspace v1 remains focused on the contract → operating decision → independent review workflow. This proposal supplies a possible future customer use case, not a reason to change the v1 scope or canonical protocol.
