# Mission Value ↔ Enterprise Value (the missing bridge)

**Canonical tension:**

> Optimize the business to fund the Mission.  
> Optimize the Mission to decide what businesses should exist.

**Danger without this layer:** a company becomes excellent at monetizing a product loosely related to the Mission, then quietly lets the product replace the Mission.

The company is not the Mission. The product is not the company. Revenue is not Mission Value. Revenue can be powerful **fuel** for Mission Value.

---

## Causal stack

```
Mission
  → Mission Value Equation          (solution-agnostic “better world”)
  → Strategic Portfolio
  → Venture / Product Value Equations
  → Cash / Capability Generation
  → Mission Reinvestment
  → Mission Progress
```

The middle bridge (portfolio → reinvestment) is constitutional, not optional.

---

## 1. Mission Value Equation

Top-level definition of better world. **Solution-agnostic.**

Example (Benchmark / healthcare workforce):

> Excellent healthcare capacity created × access × durability ÷ time × cost × effort × risk

Does **not** care whether the org ships software, consulting, hardware, agents, marketplace, or something uninvented. Products are hypotheses under it.

## 2. Enterprise Value Equation

How the **organization** creates customer value and economic strength to survive, grow, and keep advancing the Mission.

Example shape:

> Customer Value × Reach × Retention × Expansion × Margin ÷ CAC × Implementation Cost × Support Burden × Sales Cycle

This is the **economic engine** equation—not the Mission equation. Distinction is constitutional.

- Product can be economically fantastic and mission-weak.  
- Research can be mission-great and economically terrible.  
Both can belong in the portfolio—for **different, explicit reasons**.

## 3. Customer / Product Value Equation

Local to a product/service. Example Challenge Builder:

> Training quality × creator throughput × learner reach ÷ educator time × implementation effort × cost

Optimized **subject to Mission guardrails** (not multiplied into one giant equation).

## Mission Contribution Score (2×2 + Option)

Every product / service / venture / R&D program scores:

| Dimension | Question |
|-----------|----------|
| **Mission Value** | How strongly does this move the Mission equation? |
| **Enterprise Value** | How strong is this economically? |
| **Option Value** | If this works, how much does it expand future Mission potential and enterprise optionality? |

### 2×2 (Mission × Enterprise)

| | High Mission | Low Mission |
|---|--------------|-------------|
| **High Enterprise** | **Core engine** — protect & scale | **Cash engine** — allowed only with explicit justification; scrutinize |
| **Low Enterprise** | **R&D / public-good** — self-disruption lives here | **Kill** |

Option Value protects long-horizon work from short-term P&L slaughter (evidence corpus, AI-native training research, open standards, cannibalizing tech).

### Decision rule (any proposed project)

1. Does this create customer/economic value?  
2. Does this advance the Mission?  
3. Does this create future options?  

Need not score high on all three—but leadership must know **which reason justifies it**. If none → don’t do it.

---

## Causal bridge (required)

Products may not merely claim “we support the Mission.” Each declares a structured bridge:

```
Customer problem
  → Customer Value Equation
  → Mechanism chain
  → Mission Variable(s) moved
  → Expected Mission Value impact
```

Example:

**Challenge Builder**  
Customer: educators can’t repeatedly create/deliver realistic training at scale.  
Bridge: higher creator throughput → more high-quality training → more workers practice relevant skills → more competent workers → excellent healthcare capacity ↑.

If the chain weakens, Mission Council sees it immediately. **Bridge Mines** prove or falsify these links.

---

## Local optimization under parent guardrails

Product team may maximize e.g. Revenue × Retention ÷ Cost **subject to** Mission constraints:

- competency quality ≥ threshold  
- no harmful training  
- accessibility does not worsen  
- no unacceptable clinical risk  

Safer than one giant merged equation.

---

## Mission Reinvestment (formal allocation)

Explicit decision: what fraction of economic surplus goes to core growth vs long-horizon Mission advancement?

Example buckets for operating cash flow:

| Bucket | Purpose |
|--------|---------|
| **Core Growth** | sales, product, onboarding, expansion |
| **Mission R&D** | Mission advances that may cannibalize current business |
| **Open Infrastructure** | equations, standards, research, public goods |
| **Resilience** | reserves, security, legal, compliance |

Self-disruption gets a **permanent budget**, not CEO mood.

---

## “Disrupt Us” portfolio (constitutional expectation)

Every major Missionist organization maintains active work answering:

> What could make our current business obsolete while advancing the Mission faster?

Benchmark examples:

- AI coaching eliminating much scenario authoring  
- Competency assessment in workflow  
- Passive observation replacing simulations  
- Upstream education removing need for some training  
- Open marketplace outperforming owned content  

Those teams earn **MCUs for Mission advancement even if they hurt current product revenue**. Otherwise incentives protect the revenue engine forever.

---

## Mine types under this framework (Mishys)

| Type | Advances |
|------|----------|
| **Enterprise Mine** | Product/company customer or economic equation (e.g. pilot→annual 30%→50%) |
| **Mission Mine** | Higher-level Mission equation (e.g. workflow coaching vs simulation competency) |
| **Bridge Mine** | Causal link between enterprise activity and Mission outcomes (e.g. Challenge users → better independent-readiness than status quo) |

Bridge Mines keep the company honest. (Complements Outcome / Learning / Unlock Mine types.)

## Object hierarchy (Mishys model)

```
Mission
  → Mission Value Equation

Organization
  → Enterprise Value Equation
  → Mission Reinvestment policy
  → Strategic Portfolio
  → Disrupt Us portfolio

Product / Service
  → Customer Value Equation
  → Mission bridge (required)
  → Mission / Enterprise / Option scores

R&D Program
  → Mission / Option Value Equation

Mine
  → Enterprise | Mission | Bridge (+ Outcome | Learning | Unlock)
```

Each rolls upward differently. One equation does not govern everything.

Leadership sees over-investment in monetization vs under-investment in Mission progress.

---

## Anti-capture rules (constitutional)

1. **No product, service, revenue stream, customer segment, or business model may become constitutionally protected.** Products are hypotheses. The Mission is durable.  
2. **A materially superior path to the Mission must be allowed to compete with and, if proven, replace existing company products—even when that reduces existing revenue.**
