## Value equation

```
mission progress × long-term behavior × useful employee ownership × capital efficiency × resilience
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
short-term pressure × governance capture × financial fragility × forced selling × complexity
```

## Three phases

1. **Founder** — concentrated control is useful; unusual early risk; speed. A **Founder Clock** (test ~20–30 years) starts on day one, when the pie is still worth little.
2. **Contributor** — founder original ownership becomes **available** on a published schedule (**Time Mining**), **unlocked** by Mission progress (**Mission Mining**), and **earned** by contribution under rules agreed before the work. Not a calendar giveaway.
3. **Mature mission** — active contributors become the main governing constituency. Wealth already earned remains property. Permanent political control does not automatically pass to heirs or to whoever can buy the most shares.

> Time makes equity available. Mission progress unlocks it. Contribution earns it.

**Hypothesis detail (Base vs Impact, 30/10 timeline, beneficiaries vs contributors, attribution ≤100%, Capital Ledger):** [FOUNDER_CLOCK.md](./FOUNDER_CLOCK.md).

Not week-one Protocol v0.1. Must be stress-tested on real Missions.

Missionism is not anti-founder. It is anti-permanent entitlement to future value that other people create. The founder can still become extremely wealthy by making the pie much bigger. Leadership is not the same as ownership; Mission power is a third thing.

**Track three founder interests separately:** founding equity (dilutes) · contributor equity (can keep earning) · founder control rights (own clock).

**Mission Leader ≠ CEO** — long-horizon thesis vs running the company now.

Explainer: `docs/MISSIONISM.md`. Site: `/system`.

## Separate the jobs one share normally performs

| Layer | Role |
|-------|------|
| **Contributor Shares** | Economics + contributor governance, earned by contribution |
| **Capital Shares** | Economics with limited or no ordinary mission governance |
| **Mission Constitution** | Protects the mission and basic ownership rules; cannot be purchased like ordinary stock |

**One-way conversion:** Contributor → Capital for external liquidity. Capital does not convert back into Contributor governance.

> Capital can buy economics. It cannot simply buy the mission.

Non-governing capital is not unprotected capital. Outside investors still need honest reporting, anti-self-dealing rules, dilution rules, sale/liquidation treatment, and limits on changing their class economics. Accept a governance discount if it is smaller than the long-term value of protected mission control.

## Private destination, public escape hatch

Design to remain private indefinitely. Never treat IPO as graduation. List non-governing Capital Shares only when private markets cannot provide liquidity, capital, acquisition currency, diversification, or collateral at a reasonable cost.

Until then, protect the scarce resource: organizational attention directed toward the mission.

Choose the cheapest capital that does not distort the mission: retained profits → debt when cash flows can service it → Capital Shares for risk capital → public equity last.

## Anti-company-town

- Leaving must not call ordinary home/education/car loans due.
- Vested economic ownership remains the employee’s property.
- Lenders never acquire contributor governance on default.
- Employees may use outside lenders and advisers.
- Financial data use is consent-based and separated from employment decisions.
- No unlimited operating-company repurchase promise.
- Partner with regulated institutions first; integrate financial services only when scale proves it is better.

## Recession design

Avoid the run: sell → liquidity dries up → valuation falls → margin calls → more selling. Conservative advance rates. No ordinary mark-to-market margin loans for core life needs. Several liquidity sources. Cap company repurchases as a share of cash / FCF. Stress-test 50–70% valuation declines plus simultaneous employee demand.

Public listing does not automatically make employee borrowing safer — it is continuously marked to market.

## Mapping

MCUs record contribution. They are not share certificates. Never hard-code `1 MCU = X shares`. See [MCU_PROTOCOL.md](./MCU_PROTOCOL.md) and [GOVERNANCE.md](./GOVERNANCE.md).
