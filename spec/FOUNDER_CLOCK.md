## Deepest flywheel (one block)

> **Human outcome decides how much new ownership exists.**  
> **Human contribution decides who receives it.**  
> **Time guarantees the opportunity never disappears.**  
> **Impact determines how fast it happens.**  
> **Capital can finance the machine but cannot own the mission.**  
> **Past contribution is never erased, but future contribution never stops competing with it.**

---

## Bridge law

> **Beneficiary outcomes unlock equity. Contributor causation allocates it.**

Do **not** merge those concepts.

- Beneficiaries (and outcome metrics) prove whether the mission won.  
- Contributors earn the unlock according to verified causal contribution.  
- Customers / trainees are not automatic equity miners.

---

## Two MCU classes (on top of the ledger)

| Class | Meaning | When a company is “bad” |
|-------|---------|-------------------------|
| **Base MCU** | Expected useful contribution under published rules | Still earned for valid work |
| **Impact MCU** | Extraordinary causal contribution to verified mission outcomes | Acceleration / value collapse; Impact opportunity dies |

### Critical correction

> A bad company should **not** stop people from earning **Base MCUs**.  
> It should stop them from earning meaningful **Impact MCUs**.

Otherwise the founder transfers strategy risk to workers: “Sorry, my mission didn’t work, so your last five years earned no ownership.” That is anti-Missionism.

Natural punishment for a bad company:

- acceleration pools don’t unlock  
- equity is worth little  
- mission velocity is visibly terrible  
- good people leave  

You do **not** need to confiscate Base contribution.

Base also protects maintenance, security, compliance, finance, and reliability — work that rarely gets a flashy milestone.

## Timeline: boring 30 years, exciting ~10 years

### Promise (example: GoPrivate)

> **Boring economic half-life ≈ 30 years.**  
> Original founder block dilutes toward ~50% through **base mining** alone, even without heroic milestones.

Approximate annual post-mint dilution rate for a 30-year half-life of the original block: **~2.284%/year**.

| Year | Original founder block | Everyone else |
|-----:|-----------------------:|--------------:|
| 0 | 100.0% | 0.0% |
| 5 | 89.1% | 10.9% |
| 10 | 79.4% | 20.6% |
| 15 | 70.7% | 29.3% |
| 20 | 63.0% | 37.0% |
| 25 | 56.1% | 43.9% |
| **30** | **50.0%** | **50.0%** |

### Exceptional path (~10 years)

Precommitted acceleration unlocks (illustrative): **10% + 10% + 20%** post-money equity from Impact / mission goals.

After ~10 years of base mining, original block ≈ 79.37%.  
Then: `79.37% × 0.9 × 0.9 × 0.8 ≈ 51.43%`.

Tune the last unlock (~22.2%) if you want those particular milestones to land ~50% exactly.

**Psychology:**

> If we just keep building, ownership moves slowly.  
> If we crush the mission, ownership moves dramatically faster.

Much more interesting than ordinary vesting.

Exact percentages are **company constitution hypotheses**, not universal Missionism physics.

## Three interests of a founder (do not conflate)

| Interest | What happens |
|----------|----------------|
| **Founding equity** | Continuously diluted by Missionism mining |
| **Contributor equity** | Can keep earning via Base/Impact MCUs |
| **Founder control rights** | Expire on their own constitutional clock |

Example: founding stake diluted 100% → 40%; fifteen years of extraordinary Mission Leader work earns +8% contributor ownership → 48%. Fine.  
Or keep creating disproportionate value → 55%. Also fine.

Missionism is not trying to make the founder lose.  
It is trying to make **“I founded this once” stop being the source of future ownership.**

---

## Mission Leader ≠ CEO

| Role | Owns |
|------|------|
| **Mission Leader** | Long horizon: world change, honesty of the value equation, next era, mission vs company optimization |
| **CEO** | Run the organization now: P&L, hiring, firing, execution, sales, product, operating plan, capital allocation |

One person *may* hold both. Missionism must treat them as **different jobs**.

A founder can become Mission Leader / chair while a better operator is CEO.  
Eventually even Mission Leader requires ongoing confidence of mission governance — not permanent possession by founding.

Sharp jurisdiction prevents “shadow CEO” dual-boss failure.

---

## Producers vs enablers (not white/blue collar)

| Role | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Mission producers** | Directly create the beneficiary outcome |
| **Mission enablers** | Build systems that let producers create much more of the outcome |

Neither is inherently more important. The mission equation decides value **in a particular situation**. One person can be both.

## Four ledgers

| Ledger | Measures |
|--------|----------|
| **Mission Ledger** | Did reality improve? (verified mission units) |
| **Contribution Ledger** | Who helped cause it? (Base + Impact MCU / causation) |
| **Ownership Ledger** | Who owns the resulting economic pie? |
| **Capital Ledger** | Who supplied financial capital, on what terms? |

### Firewall

```text
$50M investment ≠ 50M MCUs
```

Investors get negotiated **Capital Shares** / preferred instruments.  
They may earn enormous returns and have capital protections.  
Financial capital does **not** count toward the founder→contributor Missionism transition and does **not** purchase permanent mission control.

> Capital can finance the machine but cannot own the mission.

---

## Attribution law

> **One unit of Mission Value may only be attributed once.**  
> Total causal attribution across contributors ≤ 100%.

Mission Value may be fractionally attributed down a causal tree (predeclared in Impact Contracts where possible; adjusted by evidence).  
Exact splits are **not** universal formulas.

Without this law, SMEs, engineering, sales, CS, and leadership each claim 100% of the same outcome → fake impact inflation.

Conflicted contributors (including founders scoring their own impact) need **independent attribution**.

## Acceleration: continuous throttle preferred (GoPrivate)

Giant named goals may remain on the scoreboard. Prefer **continuous Mission Velocity → mining rate** over sudden cliffs.

GoPrivate v0.1 locks floor/ceiling half-lives (30y ↔ 10y) and interpolates annually. See [missions/GOPRIVATE_MISSIONISM_V0.1.md](./missions/GOPRIVATE_MISSIONISM_V0.1.md).

If another company still uses progressive unlock “ramps,” keep durability holdbacks. Big incentives intensify Goodhart’s law — evidence quality and denominators stay part of the engine.

---

## Profit sharing = ownership; add Capital Safety

Do **not** invent a separate employee profit-sharing formula.  
If a common share owns 0.4%, it receives 0.4% of ordinary common distributions.

What Missionism needs is a **Capital Safety Rule** (company-chosen policy, locked in advance):

Example hypothesis for a founder-controlled period:

> While special founder control exists, ordinary distributions cannot exceed 50% of trailing distributable FCF and cannot leave less than 18 months of published required operating reserve; exceptions need independent/contributor approval.

Offer standardized policies (**Growth / Balanced / Yield**) rather than universal numbers.  
Cannot rewrite the policy two weeks before a giant distribution.

> Don’t cap how rich the founder can become. Prevent the founder from rigging the mechanism.

## Stress tests

### GoPrivate

**Live doctrine:** [missions/GOPRIVATE_MISSIONISM_V0.1.md](./missions/GOPRIVATE_MISSIONISM_V0.1.md).

- **Mission:** Increase global clinician autonomy  
- **Unit:** QACY (Qualified Autonomous Clinician Year)  
- **Cash:** baseline 100% clinician; above-baseline 50/50 with **buyout**  
- **Mining:** continuous 2.284%–6.697%/yr from Mission Velocity (not cliff unlocks)  
- **Pools:** 50% Clinician Lane / 50% Builder Lane  
- **Beneficiary vs builder hats:** clinicians mine Operator MCU for own QACY; Builder Impact for causing others’ QACY  

Cliff-style “hit 1M → unlock 20%” is **rejected** for GoPrivate v0.1 in favor of continuous throttle; the million remains a named scoreboard.

### Benchmark

- **Mission:** Increase global supply of excellent healthcare workers  
- **Unit:** **net additional excellent-healthcare-worker-year** (not mere completions)  
- **Gates:** role competency + incremental useful capacity + durability  
- **Beneficiary:** workers / employers / patients — not automatic equity miners  
- **Producers:** SMEs, educators, creators, qualifying partners — strong Missionism candidates  
- **Enablers:** technology, research, sales, deployment, ops  
- Harder attribution → better stress test of the system  

Benchmark unlock idea: cash + revenue share + Base/Risk MCU + Impact participation if work creates verified excellent workers — so world-class practitioners build on Benchmark, and engineers build tools that multiply them.

## Visibility psychology (hypothesis)

Failing company dashboard should make leaving cheap in information terms: low velocity, era elapsed, 0% acceleration, falling Mission Value / $, flat outcomes, near-zero projected Impact, declining equity.

Great company dashboard: era progress, targets hit, projected acceleration, rising personal Impact share, exploding Mission Value → “don’t leave.”

Employee ownership literature suggests ownership + participation + fair fixed pay matter together; treat Missionism’s exact mechanism as **experimental**, not proven.

## Known leaks and fixes

| Leak | What goes wrong | Fix |
|------|-----------------|-----|
| No Base when mission fails | Founder dumps strategy risk on workers | Valid work always earns Base; failure kills acceleration/value |
| Beneficiaries = owners | Customers clutter ownership | Outcomes unlock; contributors earn |
| Double-counting impact | Fake impact inflation | ≤100% attribution per mission unit |
| Only visible work earns Impact | Maintenance punished | Base MCU protects necessary work |
| Founder scores own impact | Permanent “most valuable” | Independent attribution when conflicted |
| Huge milestone cliff | Deadline gaming | Ramps + durability holdbacks |
| Pivot before bad era closes | Escape accountability | Close old ledger; new mission = new era |
| Easy mission replaces hard one | Unlock theater | Constitutional approval + new prospective targets |
| Investment as contribution | Buy the transition | Capital Shares only; never MCUs for cash |
| Early employee eternal aristocrat | Old ownership dominates forever | Pie keeps mining after they leave |
| Mission Leader as shadow CEO | Two bosses | Sharp mission/ops jurisdiction |
| Metric becomes target | Goodhart | Denominators, evidence, challenges, versioned equations |
