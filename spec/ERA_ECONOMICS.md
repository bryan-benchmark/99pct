# Era Economics — VC Mechanics for Human Contribution

**Status:** Core public + company economics model.  
**Companions:** [`../docs/BUILD_THE_NEXT_ERA.md`](../docs/BUILD_THE_NEXT_ERA.md) · [CERTIFICATION.md](./CERTIFICATION.md) · [FOUNDER_CLOCK.md](./FOUNDER_CLOCK.md) · [CONSTITUTION.md](./CONSTITUTION.md)

---

## Simplest explanation

> **VC says:** give us capital, get ownership.  
> **Missionism says:** create verified mission + economic value, get ownership.

Both use staged value creation, dilution, private-company valuation, milestones, and equity upside.

The major difference is **who gets newly created ownership** and **what must happen before they earn it.**

---

## The basic loop

Each company runs sequential **Eras**. An Era is expected to represent roughly **five years** of progress, but it **ends whenever the gates are actually achieved** (not on a calendar alone).

At the start of an Era, everyone knows:

| Element | Meaning |
|---------|---------|
| **Financial Gate** | Next independently certified company-value target |
| **Mission Gate** | Next independently verified mission-value target |
| **Contributor Mine** | How much of the *new aligned value* contributors can earn if both are achieved |
| **Integrity Gates** | Safety, human burden, externality, evidence, customer freedom — must PASS |

### Example — GoPrivate Era III

```text
Certified equity value at start:  $1B
Next financial gate:              $5B
New value available to create:    $4B
Contributor share s:              20%
Maximum Era III Mine:             $800M of ownership
```

Nobody gets $800M merely because valuation rises. The mission target must also be achieved with Integrity Gates intact.

---

## Governing equation

$$
\text{Contributor Mine} = s \times \text{New Verified Aligned Value}
$$

**Aligned** = economic value **and** mission value were actually created.

### Starting experiment: \( s = 20\% \)

> New generation creates $5 of aligned company value → contributors can earn $1 → existing owners retain $4 of the increase.

That is the **5:1 / Create $5 Keep $1** bargain. Exact \( s \) may vary by Era or company; Missionism should learn what produces the strongest long-term behavior. Rules are declared **before** the work.

---

## Gate psychology — mine on the weaker progress

Actual mine progress depends on the **weaker** of the two primary gates:

$$
\text{Progress} = \min(\text{Mission \%}, \text{Financial \%})
$$

| Mission | Financial | Progress toward Era Mine |
|--------:|----------:|-------------------------:|
| 30% | 80% | **30%** |
| 100% | 60% | **60%** |
| 100% | 100% + Integrity PASS | **Era won** |

When both gates are fully conquered and Integrity PASSes: Era closes, high-water marks rise, next Era and next Mine open immediately.

Behavioral box:

> Making money without accomplishing the mission doesn’t work.  
> Accomplishing the mission while destroying the company doesn’t work.  
> You have to figure out how to do both.

Integrity blocks the loophole: e.g. GoPrivate cannot inflate profits by trapping clinicians and “offset” with autonomy elsewhere.

Progressive mining on verified progress reduces cliff gaming; **full gate completion** still determines Era win.

---

## Why existing owners want dilution (productive dilution)

Company: **$1B → $5B** creates $4B of value. At \( s = 20\% \):

| Party | Approximate claim on new state |
|-------|--------------------------------|
| Contributors | $800M of ownership |
| Old owners | ~$4.2B (started at $1B) |

Old shareholder:

> “Yes, please give this generation $800M. They made the rest of us $3.2B richer.”

**Inflationary dilution** (pie shrinks or stagnates while % falls) ≠ **productive dilution** (new verified value substantially outruns dilution).

Missionism should permit dilution only when new verified value substantially outruns it.

---

## Why it is like VC

A founder may happily go from 100% of a $5M company to 70% of a $50M company — diluted and richer.

Missionism uses the same insight. Instead of repeatedly asking:

> “Who has money we can sell the next 20% to?”

the company asks:

> **Which humans can create the next enormous increment of value, and how much of that increment should we offer them to make it happen?**

Equity becomes a tool for attracting **human capability**, not primarily financial capital.

Capital still via customers, debt, bonds, finite-return financing, or equity where necessary.

---

## Why it could be better than normal VC

- Founder sells less upside merely for cash need  
- Exceptional employees get more upside than conventional employment  
- Existing employee-owners want future employees to succeed (dilution only after pie growth)  
- Teams want other teams to succeed (company-wide dual gates)  
- Customers represented via Mission Gate  

Flywheel:

```text
individual self-interest
  → team cooperation
  → company growth
  → mission progress
  → customer value
  → more employee wealth
  → better talent joins
  → repeat
```

---

## Era I is the hardest — then the institution gets better, the game gets harder

At Era I: unproven mission equation, unproven ownership promise, little value, few employees, weak liquidity, high failure risk.

Once Era I is genuinely won:

- Employees actually became owners  
- Founder demonstrated the promise is real  
- Certified valuation history exists  
- Mishys can show a completed Era  
- Future candidates see what prior contributors earned  
- Existing owners have skin for Era II  
- Next mine is worth more dollars because the company is worth more  

> **The psychological flywheel becomes easier after Era I.**  
> **Later Mission and Financial Gates should deliberately become harder.**

Avoids complacency: institution improves at winning; the game does not get soft.

## Valuation cadence (ESOP-adjacent, not quarterly theater)

Ordinary private ESOPs typically require **independent appraisal at least annually**; interim valuations when something material changes are less common. Missionism should not pay for four massive independent appraisals per year unless needed.

Recommended:

| Cadence | Role |
|---------|------|
| **Annual independent certified valuation** | Baseline / high-water mark |
| **Quarterly internal valuation roll-forwards** | Continuity for dashboards / statements |
| **Independent gate valuation** | Whenever an Era appears to have crossed its Financial Gate |

Same infrastructure can inform share statements, repurchases, liquidity, lending analysis — but lenders apply their own discounts and underwriting. One certified company value ≠ automatic loan at that value.

## Five leaks to design against

| Leak | Failure mode | Fix |
|------|--------------|-----|
| **1. Valuation gaming** | Sacrifice long-term quality for near-term financial value | Independent valuation, durable metrics, locked methodology, high-water marks |
| **2. Mission gaming** | Hit the equation, miss the outcome | Independent evidence, externalities, durability, prospective equation changes |
| **3. Gate cliff** | Stupid behavior at 95% → 100% | Progressive mining on verified progress; full gates for Era completion |
| **4. Over-hiring** | Hire to hit company gate while diluting MCU | Hire only when expected mine increase > share consumed — every hire is capital allocation |
| **5. Under-investing in long bets** | Chase next gate, starve 15-year R&D | Durability in Mission Equation; some Era targets require long-horizon capacity |

---

## Whole design (one block)

> **Start with today’s independently certified value and mission high-water mark.**  
> **Publish the next Mission Gate and Financial Gate.**  
> **Precommit what share \(s\) of new aligned value contributors can mine.**  
> **Let individuals earn shares of that mine through transparent contribution rules.**  
> **Only mine as fast as the weaker of mission and economic progress.**  
> **When both gates are fully conquered (Integrity PASS), close the Era, raise both high-water marks, and open the next mine.**  
> **Repeat forever.**

Normal VC: dilution justified because somebody brought scarce capital.

Missionism adds:

> **Dilution can also be justified because somebody created the new value that made everybody else’s shares worth more.**

Different enough to change incentives dramatically — using surprisingly conventional corporate machinery underneath.
