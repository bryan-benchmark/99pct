# Human Sustainability Constitution

**Companion to the MCU ledger — not part of MCU arithmetic.**

The ledger is rigid. The human experience must be forgiving.

This constitution does **not** change how µMCU are calculated, hashed, or verified.
It constrains how Missionism *presents*, *explains*, *compares*, and *socially interprets* contribution so the system remains livable across generations.

If the math is perfect and humans hate living inside it, Missionism still dies.

---

## Standing sentence

> **MCU must never become the mission. MCU is the receipt for serving the mission.**

---

## Stack position

```
The Human Compact              ← non-forkable dignity floor
        ↓
Human Sustainability Constitution  ← how humans live with permanent contribution
        ↓
MCU Protocol (v0.1+)           ← rigid, append-only, independently verifiable
        ↓
Product experience             ← recognition, belonging, explainability, exit
```

Fork almost anything about Missions and products.
Do **not** casually fork away the psychological invariants below without naming the failure mode you are accepting.

## Eight invariants

### 1. Earned contribution is permanent

What someone earned under published rules stays theirs.

Corrections append. Rule changes are prospective.
Never casually claw back historical MCU. Psychological ownership depends on:

> “What I earned is mine forever.”

### 2. Rules are known before work begins

Every Mission Contract states the value equation, reference schedule, and expected contribution **before** contribution starts.

Changing the scoring rule after someone has done the work should be nearly taboo.
Ambiguity is an exploit surface; pre-commitment is the defense.

### 3. Differences in reward must have explainable reasons

Every MCU issuance should be reducible to one sentence:

> “You earned 420 MCU because you contributed X under rule Y.”

If people cannot understand why someone else earned more, resentment will eventually destroy legitimacy.

### 4. People may leave without forfeiting earned contribution

Exit must be real.

Anyone can leave a mission and keep previously earned MCU.
That flips the psychology from “the institution owns me” to “I voluntarily keep contributing.”

### 5. Failure of a difficult mission is not automatically misconduct

Preserve history. Distinguish:

| Record | Meaning |
|--------|---------|
| Mission failed / hypothesis killed | Information; often still MCU-worthy |
| Person acted badly | Conduct; separate accountability path |

Difficult missions must remain worth attempting. Permanent personal stigma for honest failure is a system death condition.

### 6. Humans are never reduced to one universal score

No global human GPA. No single leaderboard of “best humans.”

MCU is mission-local economic ownership **and** a standardized contribution denomination for packages — never a worldwide rank from lifetime MCU totals.

Someone with more career MCU is not a better human. Someone with equal contribution credit does not necessarily hold equal wealth.

See [ECONOMIC_PHILOSOPHY.md](../../missionism-protocol/ECONOMIC_PHILOSOPHY.md).

### 7. Contribution creates status, but status does not create immutable privilege

Visible recognition of contribution is healthy.
Hereditary or founder supremacy is not.

Status from past contribution does not entitle someone to rewrite rules, silence challenges, or permanently outrank future contributors by birthright of tenure.

### 8. Future generations retain the same ability to earn ownership as founders had

The person born in 2475 must believe:

> **I have a legitimate path to matter here too.**

Founders get permanent credit for what they did.
They do **not** get permanent supremacy.

This is why the perpetual, expanding denominator matters psychologically as well as economically: early MCU never shrinks, but the pie keeps growing as humans keep contributing.

## Three kinds of recognition (keep separate)

| Kind | Answers | Must not become |
|------|---------|-----------------|
| **Economic** | MCU / mission ownership | “Best human” score |
| **Competence** | Repeatedly demonstrated ability on mission type/difficulty | Automatic political power |
| **Social** | Mentorship, stewardship, mission-saving work, teaching, institution-building | A second currency that secretly overrides MCU |

If whoever has the most MCU becomes the “best human,” the culture eventually poisons.

## Experience principles (product, not protocol math)

### Status without a global leaderboard

Show contribution to something meaningful:

- missions completed / served
- eras spanned
- hard missions completed
- people mentored
- long-term stewardship

Do **not** center a single global MCU ranking.

### Local comparison first

People compare locally.
Default views: similar missions, similar circumstances, similar rules.
Do not constantly show someone how far behind a founder or outlier they are.

### Autonomy over surveillance

State mission, constraints, value equation, and reward — then let people decide how.
Avoid minute-by-minute measurement cultures. MCU from proven contribution, not presence.

### Belonging over contractor extraction

Missions need identity, stories, rituals, shared history, symbols, founding moments, eras, and visible lineage.

Humans should feel: “I am part of this mission,”
not: “I am a contractor mining tokens.”

### Aspirational paths, not castes

Someone looking at a higher-contribution role should see **exactly** what they would need to demonstrate to take it.
Opaque succession is caste. Clear demonstration paths are aspiration.

### Voice without compensation democracy

People should not vote on every person’s pay.
They **should** be able to:

- challenge a rule
- flag a strange issuance
- propose a new value-equation version

### Anti-addiction / anti-casino

Missionism should feel like a **permanent civic record**, not a game.

Deliberately avoid:

- flashing token prices
- daily MCU streaks
- “you fell behind Alice this week”
- casino-style issuance animations
- endless social comparison feeds

### Anti-gaming

Do not award MCU from a single brittle metric.
Prefer explicit outputs plus a durable-quality test.
Keep equations simple enough that gaming is obvious when it happens.

## Flywheels

### Desired

```
clear mission
  → voluntary commitment
  → visible contribution
  → fair / explainable MCU
  → permanent recognition
  → stronger identity and trust
  → more contribution
  → stronger mission
  → better precedents
  → clearer/fairer future commitments
```

### Dangerous opposite

```
unclear rules
  → comparison
  → resentment
  → gaming
  → bureaucracy
  → distrust
  → political fights over MCU
  → mission becomes secondary
```

Design rule: detect when the second flywheel is starting, and prefer interventions that restore explainability, pre-commitment, exit, and mission primacy — not more surveillance or more MCU complexity.

## Relationship to Protocol v0.1

| Already enforced by ledger | Enforced by this constitution / UX |
|----------------------------|-------------------------------------|
| Append-only history | One-sentence issuance explanations |
| Prospective rule versions | Pre-work rule publication as social taboo to break |
| Mission-local pies | No global human leaderboard |
| Exit keeps MCU (nothing burns on leave) | Exit framed as dignified, not desertion |
| Corrections as new events | Clawbacks culturally illegitimate |
| Eras as display metadata | Eras as belonging / lineage, not economic reset |

**Do not move these into the calculation engine.**
AI may later help draft explanations or flag anomalies.
AI must not become the subjective MCU oracle.

---

## Explicit non-goals of this layer

- Not a personality test or virtue score
- Not democratic voting on each issuance
- Not HR performance management
- Not a replacement for The Human Compact
- Not a soft way to rewrite historical MCU

---

## Definition of done for this constitution

A future contributor can answer yes:

1. Do I understand why I earned what I earned?
2. Do I keep what I earned if I leave?
3. Can I see a path to matter more without becoming a founder’s heir?
4. Can I attempt hard work without permanent shame if the mission fails honestly?
5. Is MCU clearly a receipt for serving the mission — not the mission itself?

If those five hold for a stranger in 2475, the psychological kernel is intact.
