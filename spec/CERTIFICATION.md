# Missionism Certified Standard v1

**Status:** Draft certification standard.  
**Meaning:** Not “Missionism agrees this company is morally good.”  

> **This company has made a specific, verifiable set of commitments about mission, ownership, contribution, human burden, and transparency—and we can check whether it is honoring them.**

Certification is scalable without a central committee deciding which missions it personally likes.

Companions: [CONSTITUTION.md](./CONSTITUTION.md) · [DECISION_COMPILER.md](./DECISION_COMPILER.md) · [FOUNDER_CLOCK.md](./FOUNDER_CLOCK.md) · [MCU_PROTOCOL.md](./MCU_PROTOCOL.md) · Public: [`../docs/BUILD_THE_NEXT_ERA.md`](../docs/BUILD_THE_NEXT_ERA.md)

## Two states only

| State | Meaning |
|-------|---------|
| **Missionism Committed** | Valid Mission Constitution, `/mission`, Mission Equation, Founder Transition, first Era, contribution system — but has not yet closed a full Era under those rules. |
| **Missionism Certified** | Completed ≥1 Era and independently demonstrated ownership transferred as promised, rules followed, reporting accurate, public record matches reality. Renew annually (or at Era close). |

Do not create twelve badges.

**Certify behavior, not intentions. Certify honesty of the game, not whether the company is winning.**

A company can fail its mission (Mission Velocity −12%, acceleration 0) and remain Certified if it measured honestly and followed the system. A successful company that secretly turns off contributor mining **loses** certification.

## Requirements (all must hold while certified)

### 1. One public measurable mission

Publish one primary mission: the real-world change the company exists to make.

Normally: **Increase X** or **Decrease X**.

Not sufficient alone: increase revenue, maximize shareholder value, become #1, destroy a competitor.

Profit is how the company survives. The mission is why it deserves to survive.

---

### 2. Permanent public `/mission` page

Mandatory:

```text
company.com/mission
```

No account. No investor portal. No hidden PDF.  
Anyone can understand the basic social contract.  
Missionism’s equivalent of a public cap-table constitution.

---

### 3. `/mission` publishes Mission Arrows

Not merely the slogan — what **better** means (durable directions).

Example (Benchmark): Excellent worker-years ↑ · Competency ↑ · Access ↑ · Readiness speed ↑ · Evidence confidence ↑ · Training cost ↓ · Trainer burden ↓ · Learner burden ↓ · Safety risk ↓

Future equations may change; people can still see whether they point the same way.

---

### 4. `/mission` publishes the current Value Equation

How the company currently decides whether the mission is improving.

Must identify at least: positive outcomes, reach, duration, evidence, attribution, important human costs, externalities, hard gates.

Need not pretend every variable is perfectly measurable. **Must say where confidence is weak.**

---

### 5. Original Founder Block legally committed to contributor transition

Canonical requirement:

> **100% of the original Founder Block must be committed to eventual contributor ownership.**

Does **not** mean the founder personally must own 0%.  
The founder may later earn Contributor Equity like everyone else.  
Ownership received *because they were the original founder* goes onto a public transition clock.

Example:

```text
Original Founder Block:           100 units
Committed to Contributor Mining:  100 units
Already mined:                     14.2 units
Founder Reserve remaining:         85.8 units
```

---

### 6. Published maximum transition clock

No “someday employees will own it.”

**v1 requirement:** Founder Block transition **≤ 30 years**, with acceleration allowed.

Recommended (not mandatory exact pair):

| Path | Horizon |
|------|---------|
| Maximum / boring | ≤ 30 years |
| Exceptional | 10–15 years |

Company may choose 20/10, 25/12, 30/15, etc.

Once employees join under that maximum clock:

- It **can** move faster.  
- It **cannot** be made slower for that Founder Block.

---

### 7. Contributors receive real cash compensation

Certification must not mean: “We gave you equity, therefore we exploit your labor.”

Qualifying employees receive lawful, disclosed cash compensation under the company’s compensation system.  
Strongly recommend competitive market cash.  
Voluntary cash↔upside exchange within defined limits is allowed.  
Being wealthy enough to work for free cannot be a prerequisite for ownership.

---

### 8. Every qualifying contributor has a published ownership pathway

Before work, they must understand: **How can my contribution turn into ownership?**

v1 requires at least these categories (exact formulas may differ; cannot be secret):

| Category | Question |
|----------|----------|
| **Base Contribution** | What does doing this job competently earn? |
| **Human Exposure** | What additional burden is the company asking this person to carry? |
| **Impact** | What extraordinary improvement can earn substantially more? |

---

### 9. Human exposure measured before harm

For materially exposed roles, consider e.g.: physical danger, night work, on-call, schedule unpredictability, emotional intensity, professional liability, repetitive physical burden, loss of autonomy, or comparable costs.

> **No human burden is free just because nobody was injured this year.**

Actual injuries: ordinary legal / insurance / compensation. Observed harm also updates future exposure assumptions.

---

### 10. Locked Eras

State: Current Era start/end · mission targets · Value Equation version · contribution rules · Founder ownership available to mine · acceleration rules · hard gates.

Once the Era begins:

> **Material reward rules cannot be rewritten after seeing everyone’s performance.**

Future Eras can improve.

---

### 11. Every Era has a defined ownership release

`/mission` shows Bitcoin-like mining outcomes:

```text
Already mined:              X Founder Units
Mineable this Era:          Y Founder Units
Founder Reserve remaining:  Z Founder Units
```

Era explains: baseline unlocks A · stronger mission performance unlocks B · exceptional unlocks C.  
Underlying math may be sophisticated; humans should see outcomes.

---

### 12. Individual contribution allocates the Era release

Not merely “Employees get 3%.”

Conceptually:

```text
Your Ownership = Era Release × Your Contribution Share
```

Contribution rules are prospective.  
No individual unilaterally approves their own exceptional award.

---

### 13. Mission performance can accelerate the transition

Required conceptually (exact method may vary by mission).

Time creates the guaranteed path. Performance creates acceleration.

> **Do better together → earn the institution faster.**

---

### 14. Earned ownership is real ownership

Once vested/earned under the Era: cannot claw back merely because someone quits, is fired, disagrees with management, or changes careers.

Normal security terms still apply. Fraud may be addressed.

> **Valid past contribution remains valid.**

---

### 15. Disclose how outside capital affects Missionism structure

On `/mission`, e.g.:

```text
Founder Block: 67%
Contributor Common: 18%
Outside Capital: 15%
```

Explain: capital’s economic rights, voting/protective rights, ability to dilute contributor ownership, modify Founder Transition, or change the mission.

**Certification requirement:**

> **Outside capital cannot unilaterally turn off the Founder Transition or retroactively invalidate earned Contributor Equity.**

---

### 16. Mission Stewardship mechanism

Do not require one exact government yet.

`/mission` must state:

- Who protects the mission?  
- Who approves Value Equation changes?  
- Who approves Arrow changes?  
- Who can change future Era rules?  
- What happens if the founder / Mission Leader disappears?

May be Mission Board, Mission Steward, trust, special director, or another mechanism.

Certification checks that the mechanism **exists and follows its own rules** — not whether Missionism.com likes every decision.

---

### 17. Material mission changes get a changelog

Example:

```text
Mission Equation v4.2
Changed: durability window 9 → 12 months
Why: longitudinal evidence showed 9 months overstated persistence
Arrow affected: durability ↑
Who benefits/loses from scoring change: disclosed
Effective: Era 9 onward
Historical Eras rescored: No
```

Institutional memory.

---

### 18. Publish known weaknesses

Required `/mission` section: **What we may be wrong about**

Examples: weak evidence linking sim scores to 12-month job performance; Exposure model underprices emotional burden; Impact attribution subjective for cross-functional work.

Certification rewards honesty, not perfect equations.

---

### 19. Every Era closes with a public Mission Report

Aggregate — not employee-level confidential data.

Example fields: Mission Value · key outcomes · Human Exposure · customer value · revenue · Founder units available vs mined · contributor ownership before→after · hard gates · known regressions · Next Era response.

---

### 20. Certification must be renewed

Not once forever. Annual or aligned to Era close.

Checks include: Era closed under published rules? Promised founder ownership transferred? Value Equation changed only prospectively? Employees received recorded ownership? Capital altered protected terms? `/mission` current? Material harms disclosed?

## Ten-question onboarding application

1. What is your measurable mission?  
2. What arrows define better?  
3. What is your current Value Equation?  
4. What human/external costs does it account for?  
5. What portion of the original Founder Block is committed to contributors, and by when?  
6. What is mineable this Era, and what accelerates it?  
7. How does individual contribution determine ownership?  
8. How do you account for human exposure?  
9. Who can change the mission, equation, Era rules, and capital structure?  
10. Where can the public inspect all of this?

If answers are strong and legally/operationally real: **you’re building a Missionism company.**

## What certification is and is not

| Is | Is not |
|----|--------|
| Transparent economic commitment | Moral endorsement of the mission |
| Founders putting ownership on a clock | Guarantee of company success |
| Contributors can earn real ownership | Guarantee equity is valuable |
| Performance can accelerate transition | Central committee’s taste in causes |
| Human costs cannot be ignored | Perfect equations |
| Public inspectable rules | “Good people” badge |

Teeth without theater: **inspect the deal before you join.**
